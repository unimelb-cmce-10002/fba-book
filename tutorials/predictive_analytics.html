Tutorial 9: Predicting Baseball Game Attendance

TipLearning Goals

By the end of this tutorial, you should be able to:

  • Construct simple attendance prediction rules using historical information such as recent games and groups of similar games.
  • Apply prediction rules developed on training data to new test observations without using future information.
  • Evaluate prediction performance using MAE, RMSE, and MAPE.
  • Compare alternative prediction models using a common test sample and appropriate data reshaping.
  • Explain how different prediction rules define which historical observations are most informative.
  • Justify a model recommendation using out-of-sample evidence and communicate the result in a short analytics memo.

The Business Challenge

In the lecture, you completed an internship with the Chicago Cubs, where you helped the team predict attendance at future home games. You learned that even simple prediction rules can be useful when they use information that is available before a game takes place.

Your work with the Cubs was a success. Their cross-town rivals, the Chicago White Sox, have now hired you to help with the same problem.

Like the Cubs, the White Sox need to make decisions before each home game. How many staff should be rostered? How much food and merchandise should be available? How busy should the team expect the stadium to be? Better predictions of attendance can help the White Sox prepare for these decisions.

You already know that there are different ways we might use past games to predict attendance at a future game. For example, we could use attendance at recent games, or look at attendance for similar types of games.

The challenge is deciding which information produces useful predictions for games we have not yet observed.

Your task

Your task is to develop and evaluate several simple approaches for predicting attendance at Chicago White Sox home games.

You will:

  1. use historical White Sox games to build prediction rules;
  2. consider different ways of deciding which past games are most useful for predicting a future game;
  3. compare how well different approaches predict attendance; and
  4. communicate what you learn in a short analytics memo for the White Sox Business Analytics Team.

Exercise 8: Communicating Your Results in an Analytics Team Memo

You have now developed and evaluated several ways to predict White Sox home-game attendance.

Your task is to write a short memo for an analytics team meeting. The goal is to help the team decide which prediction approach should be used as a starting point for forecasting attendance at future games.

TipWriting Hints
  • Use the “Write Like an Amazonian” tips to guide your writing.
  • A strong memo should compare the prediction approaches, not just describe the model you prefer.
  • Focus on the analytical takeaway: what did the different prediction rules capture, and which ideas generalised to new games?
  • Use the test-set results as your main evidence when comparing predictive performance.
  • Keep the writing clear and direct. Do not simply restate the code you ran.
NoteSuggested Memo Structure

Use the outline below and provide 2–3 bullet points under each subheading.

Problem Statement
(1 short paragraph summarising why the White Sox want to predict attendance and what business decisions these forecasts could support)

Approaches Compared

  • Briefly describe the prediction approaches you explored
  • Explain what historical information each approach uses

Recommended Approach

  • Which prediction model would you recommend the analytics team use as a starting point?
  • What evidence supports your recommendation?

What We Learned About Attendance

  • What kinds of historical games appeared to be most useful for predicting attendance?
  • Did the models reveal anything about whether recent games, opponents, or other similarities matter?

Proposed Implications

  • How could the recommended predictions help with staffing, food and merchandise planning, or stadium operations?
  • How should the team interpret the size of the prediction errors when using the model?

Limitations & Next Steps

  • What are the main limitations of the prediction approach?
  • What additional information or model improvements would you want the team to explore next?
